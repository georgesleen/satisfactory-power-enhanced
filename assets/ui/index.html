    Why only one area?

    A transformer protects its own connection. Too much power heats it up; a lasting overload disconnects that area before the rest of the grid.

    Add capacity or split the load for a sustained overload. A capacitor bank helps short spikes, not a continuous shortage.

    About this sample

    The factory trips after 18 seconds of continuous overload. The accelerator trips during its third oversized peak at 22 seconds. Resetting the demo restores either connection.

    Power that does work, and power that fills the connection

    86% of a 1,000 MVA connection

    700 MW does useful work. Another 500 Mvar is reactive exchange. Together they occupy 860.23 MVA of this example connection, leaving 139.77 MVA spare.

    Power factor compares useful power with connection load: 700 / 860.23 = 0.814. This simple triangle assumes smooth sine waves. With distortion, total apparent power needs more than this triangle.

    Teaching example only. The main prototype still uses MW ratings.

    Authored curves and component amplitudes, not measured game data or FFT results. Current THD uses only the shown harmonics; voltage THD and resonance are not modeled. The filtered example reduces selected harmonics, not every possible power-quality problem.

    The MW/MWh capacitor energy buffer handles short real-power spikes. A reactive correction bank (Mvar) is different, not a universal harmonic filter and never a source of free energy. No assumptions are made about fictional machine internals.
